Remember what that shirt meant. 1958, a seventeen-year-old called Pelé. 1962, Garrincha. 1970, the most beautiful team ever. 1994, Romário. 2002, Ronaldo, Rivaldo, Ronaldinho. Five stars. No other country has five.

Then came Neymar. More goals for Brazil than anyone. More than Pelé. And Brazil became a plan with one rule. Give him the ball.

In 2014, he gets injured, and Brazil lose seven-one. In 2022, he scores against Croatia, and Brazil still go out. In 2026, he scores a penalty against Norway, and Brazil go out in the round of sixteen. Their worst World Cup since 1990.

Meanwhile, the next generation leaves early. Vinícius signs for Real Madrid at sixteen and leaves at eighteen. Endrick, the same. They grow up in Madrid, in Barcelona, in London. And they meet the national team a few times a year.

Five months before the World Cup, Raphinha is asked about it.

In June 2024, Ronaldinho says what millions of Brazilians think. "No love for the shirt. No fight. And most of all, no football." Days later, he admits it was an advertising campaign. But almost nobody argued with it.

And the results stopped arguing too. November 2023. Argentina win at the Maracanã. Brazil's first home defeat in World Cup qualifying, ever. March 2025. Argentina four, Brazil one. Then Norway, at the World Cup. Then Japan. Twice.

On November 14th, Vinícius finally scores twice for Brazil. One into the top corner. And Brazil still lose to Japan. Three-two. In stoppage time.

Romário, who won the 1994 World Cup, says it to the coach. "Ancelotti, that's enough. Pack your things and get out."

Brazil's last World Cup was twenty-four years ago. If they do not win in 2030, it will be twenty-eight. The players who will lead Brazil in 2030 have never seen their country win anything. They grew up watching seven-one. Not Yokohama.

The seven-one was supposed to be the bottom. Twelve years later, Brazil's best player is a meme. Its captain asks for rest. And Japan beat them twice.

So maybe the joke is right. Maybe something did die after the World Cup. The question is whether Brazil will notice before 2030.
