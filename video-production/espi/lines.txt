n01|In July, Real Madrid sell a striker for around forty million euros, and a few hours later they pay twenty-five million for a twenty-one-year-old who was about to sign for Hull City.
n02|And to make room for him, Mourinho leaves Endrick on the bench. Almost nobody understood it.
n03|Then, on October the fourteenth, in Rome, Real Madrid are losing three–one with ten minutes left. Mourinho looks at his bench, and he calls the kid.
n04|This isn't a story about a lucky substitute. It's a story about a decision Mourinho made in July, one that everyone treated as a mistake.
n05|Carlos Espí grows up in a small town near Valencia and comes through Levante's academy.
n06|Last season, he's named the best under-twenty-three player in La Liga, and his goals help Levante pull off a survival nobody expected.
n07|But his future isn't in Madrid. Brighton want him, and Hull City, just promoted to the Premier League, are already negotiating.
n08|Then Real Madrid call. Levante stop the Hull deal on the spot, Madrid pay the release clause within hours, and a deal that took weeks to build in England is dead in an afternoon.
n09|Weeks later, Mourinho explains how it happened.
n10|The reaction is confusion. Real Madrid already have Mbappé, they already have Endrick, so why spend twenty-five million on a kid from Levante the same day you sell your backup striker?
n11|And then Endrick gets injured in pre-season. By the time he's back, Espí is ahead of him, and by late September Endrick has played four minutes all season.
n12|In November, with Brazil, he says it out loud: I just want to play.
n13|Even Carlo Ancelotti, now Brazil's coach, publicly complains about Europe's biggest clubs not giving young South Americans enough minutes. The question is everywhere: why him, and not Endrick?
n14|What almost nobody knows is that Mourinho didn't just sign Carlos Espí. He lives with him.
n15|Mourinho sees him every day, up close, and he describes a kid who knows exactly what is happening to him.
n16|Thibaut Courtois sees the same thing in training.
n17|That's the point. Real Madrid didn't have a player like him: a one-metre-ninety-four number nine who lives in the box and fights centre-backs for fifteen minutes.
n18|Mourinho wasn't buying a starter. He was buying a weapon for the last quarter of an hour.
n19|August the twenty-second. Mourinho's first league game back, Espanyol away, one–one. Espí comes off the bench, and in stoppage time, on his league debut, he scores the winner.
n20|September the fifteenth, in Elche. Real throw away a two-goal lead, and in stoppage time Bellingham drives forward, Mbappé squares it, and Espí finishes. Three–two.
n21|October the fourteenth. Rome. Three–one down. Espí comes on and scores Real's second, and minutes later it's four–three, Real Madrid.
n22|And on November the twenty-first, at the Bernabéu, Celta are ahead until Bellingham levels, and in the eighty-fifth minute Espí follows up his own header for the winner. His fourth goal, and Real Madrid's ninth win in a row.
n23|And here's what people miss. Mourinho never gave up on Endrick. When offers arrive, Florentino Pérez backs him and refuses to sell.
n24|This was never Espí against Endrick. It was a bet only Mourinho could see, and he was willing to look like the villain in Endrick's story to make it.
n25|In July, Endrick was the name everyone wanted to see. Espí was the one only Mourinho saw. So far, Mourinho is right.
