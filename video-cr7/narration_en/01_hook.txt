On top, Cristiano Ronaldo, sitting next to Jorge Jesus. Below, Homelander. Same sideways glance. Same half-smile of a man who has just been contradicted and is working out how much longer he can keep quiet. That's what the internet sees in both of them: the most powerful man in the room, who can't handle the word "no."
Homelander flies, takes down planes and shoots lasers out of his eyes, but he loses control the moment somebody else makes a decision for him. And Cristiano, who in twenty years won everything a footballer can win, spent the last four fighting every coach, club and executive who dared to tell him to wait his turn.
Today, November fifth, twenty twenty-six, Cristiano Ronaldo announced he will never play for Portugal again. Five weeks after walking out of the national team camp in the middle of an international break, at war with the coach he himself had recommended for the job.
The all-time top scorer in international football, a hundred and forty-six goals in two hundred and thirty-four games, said goodbye without a farewell match, reading a letter in a phone video. And to understand how it came to this, we have to go back four years.
