After the World Cup, the Portuguese federation hired Jorge Jesus. The same coach Cristiano had brought to Al Nassr, and who, according to the player himself, he had recommended to the federation's president. In August, the two of them sat down and agreed on a plan: in the September international window, Cristiano would play the first game and the last.
In the first game, against Wales, he started, came off in the second half, and before leaving the pitch, he put the captain's armband on Bruno Fernandes's arm with his own hands. On the day of the game in Norway, Jesus called Cristiano aside at lunch and asked if he could handle thirty minutes. He said yes. He warmed up from half-time, watched the clock run, watched João Félix and Gonçalo Ramos score the winning goals, and never came on. At the end, he left the pitch without acknowledging the fans.
Two days later there was a meeting. And this is where the story gets a little too close to the show. According to the statement Cristiano himself published later, it was agreed that Jesus would open his press conference by reading points from a text approved by the player. And one of those points said the coach had been wrong to have someone of the stature of Pelé or Maradona warm up and then not come on.
Jesus read none of it. He told the press conference that the coach decides who plays. And an hour later, Cristiano walked out of the camp in Copenhagen and wrote on Instagram that, in due time, he would tell everyone the truth.
In The Boys, the company writes everything Homelander says in public. And the whole series is about the day he decides he doesn't need a script anymore. In real life, it went the other way around: it was the hero writing the script for the coach to read.
