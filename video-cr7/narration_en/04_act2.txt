From England, Cristiano went to Saudi Arabia, where he'd finally have what he always wanted: an entire league revolving around him. And for three years, that's exactly what happened. Until, in the January window of twenty twenty-six, Al Hilal, Al Nassr's biggest rival, signed Karim Benzema. And Cristiano concluded that the fund controlling the country's four biggest clubs was placing its bets somewhere else.
His answer was to stop playing. He sat out three games in a row, no injury, no fitness issue, in open protest against the fund. Meanwhile the league fired warnings through the press, and a Saudi TV host pointed out that no Manchester City player would ever refuse to take the field to protest against the club's owner. A week later, he came back as if nothing had happened.
Something very similar happens in season two of the show. A new hero joins the team, starts blowing up on social media, the company starts betting on her, and the most powerful man in the world behaves like someone who has just been replaced by a newer model.
