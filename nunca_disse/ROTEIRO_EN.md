## 1. "Follow your heart" (Matt 15:18–19; Jer 17:9)

> The first one is the most famous of all: "follow your heart."
> It sounds beautiful. It sounds wise. The problem is what Jesus said about the heart.
> One day, the religious leaders complained that the disciples were eating without washing their hands. To them, the dirt came from the outside.
> Jesus answered: it's not what goes into the mouth that defiles a man. It's what comes out.
> "For out of the heart proceed evil thoughts, murders, adulteries, thefts, false witness."
> He didn't say the heart is a guide. He said it's the source of the problem.
> Centuries earlier, the prophet Jeremiah had already written: "The heart is deceitful above all things."
> Notice: Jesus doesn't tell you to hate your heart. He tells you to stop obeying it, and to give it to the One who can make it new.
> Tell me in the comments: how many times have you heard "follow your heart"? I read every single one.

## 2. "Just be happy" (John 16:33; Matt 5:4)

> The second one: "all that matters is being happy."
> Now look at Jesus' last night with His disciples. He knew that in a few hours, He would be arrested.
> And instead of promising them an easy life, He said:
> "In the world ye shall have tribulation…"

*(1 second of silence)*

> "…but be of good cheer. I have overcome the world."
> Jesus never sold a life without pain. He promised His presence in the middle of it.
> The peace He offers isn't the absence of the storm. It's knowing who's in the boat with you.
> And there's a detail in the Sermon on the Mount. Some translations begin the Beatitudes like this: "Happy are those who mourn."
> Jesus wasn't against happiness. He just changed where it's found.
> If even one of these lies has ever run your life, you're not alone. On this channel, every week there's a video like this one: what the world says, and what the Bible really says. Subscribe and turn on the bell, so you don't miss the next one.

*Source:* Matt 5:4 in the Good News Translation: "Happy are those who mourn; God will comfort them!"

## 3. "Only love those who love you" (Matt 5:43–46; Luke 23:34)

> The third one is quieter, but it runs a lot of lives: "only love those who love you."
> Pay them back in their own coin. Whoever hurt you doesn't deserve a thing.
> In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus quoted a saying of His day: "Thou shalt love thy neighbour, and hate thine enemy."
> And He went on: "But I say unto you, love your enemies, and pray for them which persecute you."
> Then He asked an uncomfortable question: "If ye love them which love you, what reward have ye?"
> Loving the people who love you, anyone can do that. It doesn't take God.
> Now the detail: the second half of that saying, "hate thine enemy," isn't in the Law. The Law only said to love your neighbour. The hate was a popular add-on.
> And Jesus didn't just teach it. On the cross, He prayed for the men nailing Him there: "Father, forgive them; for they know not what they do."

## 4. "You have to be in control" (Matt 6:25–34)

> The fourth one is our generation's motto: "you have to have everything under control."
> Spreadsheets, goals, plan B, plan C. And the anxiety at three in the morning, when none of it guarantees anything.
> Jesus looked at the crowd and pointed to the sky: "Behold the fowls of the air: they sow not, neither do they reap, yet your heavenly Father feedeth them."
> Then He asked: "Which of you by taking thought can add one cubit unto his stature?"
> And He concluded: "Take no thought for the morrow. Sufficient unto the day is the evil thereof."
> He didn't tell us to be irresponsible. He told us to stop carrying a day that hasn't even come yet.
> Notice where this is: in the same sermon as the Lord's Prayer. "Give us this day our daily bread."
> Not this month's bread. Today's. Even the prayer He taught takes tomorrow out of your hands.

## 5. "You don't need to change" (Mark 1:15; Luke 9:23; John 8:11)

> The fifth one sounds like love: "you don't need to change. Just be yourself."
> But do you know what Jesus' first public words were, in the Gospel of Mark?
> "Repent ye, and believe the gospel." To repent is to change direction.
> Later, He said: "If any man will come after me, let him deny himself, and take up his cross daily, and follow me."
> It's not "be yourself." It's "deny yourself."
> But look at how He does it. They brought Him a woman caught in adultery, ready to stone her.
> Jesus said: "Neither do I condemn thee." And then: "Go, and sin no more."
> First the welcome. Then the change. Jesus accepts you as you are, but He loves you too much to leave you that way.
> If you've made it this far, you're one of the few who watch to the end. And the last one is the most dangerous. If you haven't subscribed yet, now's the time.

## 6. "You can do it on your own" (John 15:5; Matt 26:33–35; John 21:15–17)

> The last one is the proudest: "you can do it on your own. You don't need anyone."
> On the night He was betrayed, Jesus was walking with His disciples, and He talked about a vine.
> "I am the vine, ye are the branches. Without me ye can do nothing."
> Nothing. A branch that's been cut off doesn't get stronger. It dries up.
> That night, one disciple thought he could. Peter said: "Though I should die with thee, yet will I not deny thee."
> Hours later, he denied Jesus three times. And the rooster crowed.
> But the story doesn't end there. After the resurrection, on a beach, Jesus asked Peter: "Lovest thou me?"
> Three times. One for each denial.
> The Peter who trusted in himself fell. The Peter who depended on Jesus preached to thousands.
