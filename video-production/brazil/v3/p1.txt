July 8th, 2014. Belo Horizonte. In twenty-nine minutes, Germany score five goals against Brazil. At home. In a World Cup semi-final. For twelve years, that was the lowest point in Brazilian football. It was supposed to be the bottom.

It wasn't. Twelve years later, the joke got worse.

This summer, Vinícius Júnior has a cosmetic procedure on his chin. His face comes back different. And the internet decides he did not survive. "Vini died after the World Cup." "That's a body double."

That is the joke about his face. The other joke is about his football. In January, the Bernabéu whistles him. In the autumn, it whistles him again. Mourinho and Mbappé have to defend him in public. The best Brazilian player of his generation, whistled by his own fans.

He is not the only one. Raphinha is Brazil's captain. One of the best players in Europe. June 2025. Barcelona are not at the Club World Cup, and Raphinha is already on holiday. He says this about the players who are there. "It's hard to give up your holidays. Not everyone will want to go."

He is talking about clubs, not Brazil. But Zico answers him anyway. "If he had played that tournament during his holidays, he wouldn't have said that."

Seventeen months later, before a trip to Asia with Brazil, Raphinha says this about his own coach. "If he really counts on me, he knows he has to manage my minutes."

He has been injured three times in eight months. But in Brazil, nobody hears the injuries. They hear a player choosing which games to play for his country. Days later, at a press conference in Singapore, he tries to explain.

For twenty-four seconds, he says nothing. Then he wears the armband against Japan, and he is sent off.

Now look at the men who used to wear that armband. Dunga in 1994, screaming at the sky with the trophy in his hands. Cafu in 2002, on the podium in Yokohama. Shaved heads. No cameras in the dressing room. No skincare brand.

Today's Brazil has tattoos on every arm, a hairstylist before every tournament, and more followers than any team on Earth. And it is easy to say that is the problem.

But in 2002, Ronaldo walks out with the worst haircut in World Cup history. A triangle of hair on a shaved head. He scores eight goals. Two in the final.

So the problem was never the hair. Back then, the image came after the trophy. Now it comes instead of it.
